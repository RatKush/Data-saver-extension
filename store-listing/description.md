# Store listing description

Paste into the "Description" field on the Chrome Web Store dashboard.
The first ~2 lines show before "Read more" is clicked, so they carry
the most weight.

---

Data Saver cuts how much data a webpage downloads by blocking ads,
trackers, images, and autoplaying or streaming video — turn all three
on and you're browsing in a lightweight, text-only mode. Built for
anyone on a limited, slow, or metered connection.

**What it blocks**

🚫 **Ads & trackers** — a maintained blocklist of known ad and tracking
domains, plus pattern-based rules for ad networks and analytics
scripts, all evaluated locally by Chrome — nothing is sent to any
server to make a blocking decision.

🖼️ **Images** — loads pages as text-only. Blocked images collapse
cleanly instead of leaving broken-icon boxes behind.

🎬 **Video & audio** — stops autoplay before it starts, and blocks the
adaptive-streaming (HLS/DASH) requests that most video sites use, not
just old-style direct video downloads — so streaming sites actually
stop consuming bandwidth, not just visibly-playing `<video>` tags.

**See what you're saving**

📊 **Savings meter** — the popup shows how many ads, images and videos
have been blocked and roughly how much data that saved. Counted on your
device from what actually got blocked; nothing is sent anywhere.

**Sensible out of the box**

▶️ **Streaming sites work straight away** — YouTube, Netflix, Instagram,
TikTok, Twitch and other video platforms ship unblocked. You already know
those cost data; blocking them just stops them working. Want to save data
on one anyway? A single tap turns blocking back on for it.

**Per-site rules**

🎛️ Block ads on a site but let its images through — or the reverse. Any
site can have its own combination, and it sticks. Subdomains are covered
automatically.

**Optional extras**

🍪 **Cookie banners answered for you** — picks "reject" or "necessary
only" wherever a banner offers it. It never accepts on your behalf: if no
private option can be found, the banner is simply hidden and nothing is
agreed to.

🚪 **Pop-up blocking** — stops windows a page opens on its own. Windows
you open by clicking still work.

📶 **Adapt to connection speed** — stop blocking images when you're on a
fast connection, using your browser's own speed estimate. Nothing is
measured or sent.

🪫 **Data budget** — enter your monthly allowance and billing date, and
Data Saver blocks progressively harder as the cycle runs down. It does
not measure your usage and never claims a figure — no extension can see
that — it simply paces from the two numbers you gave it, and always shows
which stage it's on with one tap to ease off.

**Where your savings came from**

📈 The dashboard shows a 14-day trend and, if you switch it on, which
sites saved you the most. That site list is off by default, stays on your
device, and is deleted the moment you switch it off.

**For schools and companies**

🏢 Settings can be pinned centrally by policy, and any configuration can
be exported as a file and deployed to a fleet.

**Built for control, not all-or-nothing**

- Three independent toggles — turn off just images, just video, or just
  ads, however you want to balance data savings against how a page
  looks.
- **One tap to unblock a site** — the first thing in the popup is a
  button that stops all blocking on the site you're on, without
  touching your settings anywhere else. Or press Alt+Shift+D without
  opening anything. The toolbar icon shows "OFF" on sites you've
  unblocked, and your choice sticks until you change it.

**Privacy**

Data Saver collects nothing. No analytics, no tracking, no server of
its own. Every blocking rule ships inside the extension and runs
entirely inside your browser. Full privacy policy: [link]

*Note: this file describes what ships in v2.3 — update it if the blocking
behaviour changes materially in a future version, so the listing stays
accurate.*

*Two things never to overstate, because neither is measurable and the
product's credibility rests on not pretending otherwise:*

- *The savings meter is an ESTIMATE derived from blocked-request counts.
  Chrome exposes no per-request byte total to extensions. Don't describe
  it as a measurement.*
- *The data budget does NOT track consumption and must never be described
  as showing data remaining. It paces from the allowance and the date
  only.*

*Also don't overstate the cookie-banner feature: it answers banners it
recognises and hides ones it can't, and it never accepts. Claiming it
"removes all cookie banners" would be wrong.*
