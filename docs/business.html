The claim we will not make

Most tools in this category will quote you a percentage. We will not, and it is worth explaining why, because it is the same reason the product is worth deploying.

Chrome exposes no API that tells an extension how many bytes a blocked request would have transferred. Anyone quoting you a precise saving from inside a browser extension is estimating and presenting it as measurement. Your own network already measures this properly. So the offer is a pilot: deploy it to a slice of your fleet, read your own netflow or bandwidth reporting, and decide on your numbers rather than ours.

A 30-day pilot costs nothing and ends with a figure from your own monitoring.

Who this is for

Mobile operators & capped-market ISPs

Bandwidth is a direct cost line. Reducing page weight on subscriber devices reduces it at the source rather than at the network edge.

Schools & universities

Shared links that collapse at 9am. Blocking images and video on managed lab machines frees capacity without new hardware.

NGOs and field operations

Satellite and mobile-backhauled sites where every megabyte is metered and the connection is the constraint on the work.

Enterprises paying roaming

Travelling staff on tethered or foreign-network connections, where a single laptop can generate a surprising invoice.

Device makers

Entry-level hardware shipped into markets where data cost, not device cost, is what limits use. Ships preconfigured.

What a licence covers

  • Central deployment and lock-down. Every setting can be pinned by policy through Group Policy, MDM, or the Google Admin console. Locked settings are shown to the user as managed rather than silently enforced.
  • The maintained blocklist. Roughly 3,600 rules, rebuilt from upstream sources and shipped inside the package — no runtime fetch, so nothing breaks when your firewall blocks an update host.
  • Per-site rules. Block ads on a site while allowing its images, or the reverse, pinned centrally so an internal application keeps working.
  • Portable configuration. Work out a configuration on one machine, export it as JSON, and deploy that same file as policy.
  • A named contact for deployment questions during the pilot and the licence term.

Why it is safe to deploy

  • Manifest V3, no remote code. Every blocking rule ships inside the package and is evaluated by Chrome itself. The extension never fetches executable content.
  • No network egress of its own. No analytics, no telemetry, no licence call-home. There is nothing for your egress filtering to allow.
  • No data collection. Settings and blocked-request counts stay on the device. The one feature that records hostnames is off by default and local. See the privacy policy.
  • Public source. Every claim on this page can be checked against the code rather than taken on trust.
  • Already in production. Thousands of weekly users through the Chrome Web Store, which is the same build you would deploy.
